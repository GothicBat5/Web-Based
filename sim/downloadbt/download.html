What is color theory?

Color theory is a set of guidelines for understanding how colors relate to each other and how people respond to them. Artists, designers, photographers, and anyone who picks paint for a room use it to choose colors that work together instead of guessing.

It comes down to three questions. What are the basic building blocks of color? How can they be described precisely? Which combinations look balanced, and which create tension?

The color wheel

The color wheel arranges hues in a circle so the relationships between them are easy to see. The traditional version starts with three primary colors, which can’t be made by mixing others. Mixing two primaries gives a secondary color, and mixing a primary with its neighboring secondary gives a tertiary color.

Red, yellow, and blue (primary), then orange, green, and violet (secondary).

Hue, saturation, and value

Any color can be described with three properties.

Hue

The color itself, such as red, green, or blue: its position on the wheel.

Saturation

How intense or pure a color is. Low saturation looks muted and grayish; high saturation looks vivid.

Value

How light or dark a color is. Adding white makes a tint, and adding black makes a shade.
